Rendering runs on your machine, which needs Python 3.9+, Node.js 26 and npm; the agent installs everything else.

One template, every new film

The scenes, layouts, timing and checks are built once. A new film changes only what belongs to it.

  Built onceScene structure and camera moves · layouts for 16:9 and 9:16 · beat-planned timing · render checks

  Changed per filmPhotos · names and dates · every line of copy · music and format

vvv.py describe tells the agent each template's photo slots, copy fields and settings, and validate catches bad inputs before a render. The agent changes the template's inputs first and its React/TypeScript source only where the brief needs it. Reuse is meant to save the tokens otherwise spent redesigning, regenerating and debugging the same video code; no comparative benchmark is published yet (see the roadmap).

Show it a film you love. Get your own.

Give your agent a reference video and the attachments that make it yours: photos, a logo, copy, music. It studies how the reference works and rebuilds it as an editable project with your material.

    01 Reference + attachments
A local video file plus your photos, logo, copy and music. The reference is studied, never shipped.
    02 Measure
Sections in beats, shot lengths, still frames and beat alignment, measured from the actual file.
    03 Rebuild
On the closest template or a new composition. Missing artwork is generated; the result is checked against the reference.

Use the vvv skill to recreate ./reference.mp4 as our own film with the photos in ./photos, our logo and copy. Generate any missing artwork and show me a checked sample before the full render.

Best with an image-capable agent. With Claude Code and Codex both installed, Claude Code can use Codex's image generation through the bundled bridge. Video and voice are not generated. How it works

Also included

- Photos in emotional order: every template maps its emotional sections (laughs before tears, first date to wedding). The agent looks at each photo, tags who is in it, its mood and when it was taken, places it in the matching section, and shows you the order before rendering.
- Style presets: settle the opening rule, the music and the studio name once, save them, and every later film starts from them.
- Render checks: vvv check measures format, audio, still stretches and beat alignment, then lists what only a viewer can judge.
- Handoff: pack a template, effect or finished project as one .vvv file with its code, media licenses and render evidence, so any agent can continue it.
- Footage tools: a local FFmpeg helper probes media, cuts ranges, extracts audio and samples frames (walkthrough).
- Community templates: built one you are proud of? Pack it and submit it on the website. Once approved, it appears on the site and in the MCP server.

Your agent runs the whole thing

You describe the film, watch the sample and say what to change. Setup, scripts, rendering and checks are the agent's job:

    01 Pick a template
Matched to your format, style and photo count, such as Album · 9:16 for a phone-first short.
    02 Place the photos
Checks their size and orientation, then fills the opening, gallery and ending.
    03 Write the copy
Names, date and every line of text, validated before anything renders.

    04 Set up and sample
Installs dependencies, fetches the music and renders a sample for you to watch.
    05 Check
Measures format, loudness, still frames and beat sync, then reviews the frames.
    06 Deliver
The film plus its editable project, so any agent can pick it up later.

The first time your agent runs commands on your machine, it may ask for permission. To run things by hand or build a new template, see getting started and contributing; ready-made .vvv template packages are attached to Releases.
