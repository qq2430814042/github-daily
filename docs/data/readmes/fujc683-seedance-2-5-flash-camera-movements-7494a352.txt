Why camera language matters for video models

A text-to-video model has to invent motion. If you do not specify the camera,
it picks one for you — usually a slow push-in. Naming the move gives you control.

The 12 moves worth knowing

| Move | Prompt phrase | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Dolly in | slow dolly-in toward the subject | reveal, tension |
| Dolly out | dolly out to reveal the full scene | context, endings |
| Orbit | slow orbit around the product | product, hero shots |
| Tracking | tracking shot following the runner | action, motion |
| Crane up | crane up over the skyline | scale, establishing |
| Handheld | handheld, subtle shake | documentary, realism |
| Whip pan | whip pan to the second subject | transitions |
| Tilt up | tilt up from feet to face | character reveal |
| Push-through | push through the doorway into the room | immersion |
| Drone reveal | aerial drone pull-back revealing the valley | landscape |
| Static | locked-off static camera | product, dialogue |
| Rack focus | rack focus from foreground flower to subject | depth |

Prompt template

subject + action], [camera move], [lens], [light], [pace]

Example:

a ceramic mug rotating on a pedestal, slow orbit, 85mm, hard rim light, 24fps

Mistakes that waste renders

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Two camera moves at once | pick one, or split into two shots |
| No subject motion | add what the subject is doing |
| "cinematic" alone | name lens + light + pace |
| 30 s first try | draft at 4 s |
